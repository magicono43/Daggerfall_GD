# MOVE architecture review

This review covers MOVE-001 through MOVE-027 in the [feature catalog](../systems/MOVE_FEATURE_CATALOG.md). It uses static inspection of the local DFU 1.1.1 Release snapshot. The [repository overview](../00_REPOSITORY_OVERVIEW.md) records revision limits. Source paths below are relative to Assets/Scripts. Code ordering and dependencies are confirmed where stated. Physics outcomes, serialized tuning, classic parity and mod combinations require fixtures.

## Existing architecture

### Input, bindings and view · MOVE-001–003

Game/InputManager.cs centralizes device polling, action edges, axes, controller cursor movement and serialized bindings. PollInput feeds ActionStarted and ActionComplete. SetBinding and GetComboCode own binding interpretation, while SaveKeyBinds and LoadKeyBinds persist the profile and publish hooks. This action vocabulary lets consumers share bindings. The singleton and Unity KeyCode/axis dependencies tie capture, remapping and gameplay queries together. Frame-edge delivery to fixed steps and clearing on modal or load boundaries are important contracts. The reviewed paths do not establish missed or repeated input in play. Game/PlayerMouseLook.cs handles look, smoothing and facing, while PlayerActivate.Update uses view/cursor context for targeting. Preserve camera responsiveness and saved yaw/pitch without making render smoothing authoritative for collision.

### Ground, collision and feedback · MOVE-004–010, 017

Game/PlayerMotor.cs FixedUpdate derives grounded state from collision flags, handles cancellation and freeze, runs head/front probes, evaluates rappel/climb eligibility and then either yields to another motor or processes ground/air movement. It delegates speed adjustment to PlayerSpeedChanger, slopes and friction to FrictionMotor, jumps and gravity to AcrobatMotor, geometry probes to PlayerMoveScanner and platform movement to PlayerGroundMotor. This decomposition keeps the main order visible. Several helpers exchange mutable movement vectors and controller state. A reordered probe or controller move can change later collision results even with identical input.

PlayerHeightChanger.DecideHeightAction combines water, crouch, riding and climb state. Its Update-driven transitions adjust both collider and camera, with different ordering when crouching versus standing. Head clearance is therefore a physics and presentation contract. GroundMotor can issue a platform adhesion move in addition to the main move. PlayerMotor.OnControllerColliderHit feeds collision handling, while PlayerCollisionHandler and PlayerCrush bridge contact to gameplay. Preserve intentional multiple collision operations where fixtures require them.

AcrobatMotor tracks fall start and clears it on movement cancellation. It uses a string-based Unity SendMessage with DontRequireReceiver to notify PlayerFootsteps.ApplyPlayerFallDamage. A missing receiver can silently suppress the damage handoff. PlayerFootsteps and HeadBobber already specialize in feedback, but locating damage behind a feedback component couples gameplay consequences to scene wiring. See Game/Player/AcrobatMotor.cs, Game/PlayerFootsteps.cs and Game/Player/HeadBobber.cs. Measure probe counts and platform-heavy scenes before optimizing.

### Climb, water, levitation and transport · MOVE-011–016

ClimbingMotor owns wall eligibility, skill checks, motion and advanced climbing save fields. RappelMotor owns edge-entry checks and reset. PlayerMotor evaluates rappel before climbing when on foot, then returns early if climbing or a swim/levitate mode is active. ClimbingMotor can move the controller directly. LevitateMotor.Update drives swim/levitation through PlayerGroundMotor on the frame clock, distinct from ground movement's fixed clock. Its stationary path issues six small moves to remain responsive to moving objects, then the normal move. The extra calls are confirmed. Their cost and replacement behavior need profiling and collision fixtures.

PlayerEnterExit.Update reads dungeon block water and applies different entry/exit height checks. PlayerHeightChanger reacts to water and riding, and PlayerFootsteps handles splashes. LevitateMotor.AddMovement handles water walking, sinking and restrictions near the surface. Separate surface and submersion thresholds help avoid chatter. Mode selection spans world observations, movement flags, effect state and presentation. Overlapping climb/swim/levitation can be hard to reproduce without testing both clocks and transition order. Game/TransportManager.cs owns mount eligibility and transition handlers, including inventory and ship concerns. Keep transport policy distinct from collider and camera height.

### Targeting and activation · MOVE-018–025

Game/PlayerActivate.cs Update chooses a ray, performs the hit query and executes ordered checks for quest resources, buildings and doors, mechanisms, loot, boards, NPCs and other fixtures. Non-person quest resources can receive a click before built-in activation, subject to distance and Info mode. Some earlier branches return before later checks. The code also invokes a registered model/flat callback and then IPlayerActivable on the hit transform when each matches. Both can run for one ray hit when control reaches them. This is confirmed composition. Whether existing mods double-apply a consequence is unknown.

PlayerActivate also owns access checks and execution for exterior locks and trespass, action doors and open/lock effects, ladders and shelves, property/wagon prompts, NPC dialogue and pickpocketing. It reaches into world directory data, quest resources, item/loot state, formulas and UI. Its central order is valuable, but adding a new handler risks changing unrelated paths. A ray hit or picker callback can outlive the context used to show a prompt. Confirm target validity and access at commit where actions defer. RegisterCustomActivation uses model/flat-derived names and sends a Unity RaycastHit to callbacks. This is a lightweight extension seam, with scene naming and engine physics exposed as its public contract. See ActivateStaticDoor, ActivateActionDoor, ActivateLootContainer, ActivateStaticNPC, Pickpocket and HandleRegisterCustomActivation in PlayerActivate, and Internal/DaggerfallAction.cs.

### Traversal, placement and persistence · MOVE-026–027

Game/PlayerEnterExit.cs owns building and dungeon entry/exit, world parent activation, water context and spawn restoration. Its transition events distinguish entry attempts, success and some failures. RespawnPlayer deregisters serializable objects and starts Respawner. The coroutine waits a frame, updates GPS and streaming coordinates, selects exterior, dungeon or building reconstruction, then clears IsRespawning and signals completion. It has an exterior fallback for unmatched context. The building branch checks exteriorDoors for null before indexing element zero. That guard alone permits an empty array. Upstream caller constraints and runtime reachability require validation. The exterior wait on world.IsInit has no local timeout, and exceptions before completion can bypass flag clearing.

Game/Serialization/SerializablePlayer.cs records transform, world coordinates, compensation and floating-origin version, view, crouch, location context and transport. Game/Serialization/SaveLoadManager.cs restores position and advanced climbing state, waits on IsRespawning, and then applies restored object state. This separates world reconstruction from most object restoration. The frame wait and boolean imply readiness without a failure result, so MOVE and SAVE need a contract for failed placement and climbing geometry that no longer exists. PlayerMotor.FindGroundPosition and FixStanding are useful explicit recovery operations. Their geometry and fallback limits need fixtures.

## Comparison

| Concern | DFU | Proposed Godot design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input and view | Shared polling and bindings with frame consumers | Explicit action edges and intents with render-only smoothing |
| Movement | Specialized motors with frame/fixed handoffs and controller moves | One arbitration point with specialized mode calculations and ordered collision operations |
| Interaction | Raycast followed by ordered checks and engine-bound extensions | Typed target and intent with explicit consumption, notification and domain ownership |
| Placement | Transition events, coroutines and readiness flags | Explicit placement completion and versioned restore context |

Verify action edges through pause and physics catch-up. Exercise steps, slopes, clearance, moving platforms, fall resets, climb/rappel edges, water thresholds and levitation transitions. Test overlapping activation handlers, cancelled prompts and stale targets. Verify spawn fallbacks, readiness failures and restoration with changed geometry or missing markers. No runtime, profiling or classic parity tests were performed.
