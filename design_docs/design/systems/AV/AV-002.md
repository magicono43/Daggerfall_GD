# AV-002 — Billboard batching and animation

**Approval status — Approved, finalized v1.** The user approved Draft v1 on 2026-11-16 with “I approve”. See AV-002-A01 in the [gameplay decision log](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#av-002-interview-decisions).

## Gameplay purpose and intended player experience

Present world scenery sprites at recognizable sizes and stable anchors, with clean animation, readable silhouettes and consistent placement as the player moves. Decorative animals and lights can loop independently. Authored groups can animate together.

## Confirmed requirements

[AV-002-D01 and D02](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#av-002-interview-decisions) confirm the stock rates for the inspected batch families, stable decorative phase offsets, moddable timing and grouping, and both batched and independent rendering. AV-002-A01 approves the complete specification.

## Existing Daggerfall Unity behavior

In the local DFU 1.1.1 release snapshot, `Assets/Scripts/Internal/DaggerfallBillboardBatch.cs#SetMaterial` configures a batch from an atlas and assigns 5 fps for the stock animal archive, 12 fps for the stock light archive and zero for other archives on that path. The custom-material overload sets the batch animation rate to zero. `AddItem` can choose a random starting frame. `AnimateBillboards` schedules UV updates over existing quad geometry. These findings describe the inspected batch path. See the [AV architecture review](../../../analysis/architecture/AV_ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md).

## Detailed rules and behaviors

1. Use the validated imagery, frame structure, size, orientation policy and anchor for each placed billboard. Keep its authored anchor stable across frames. Apply the supported camera-facing rule at presentation time. Keep placement identity independent of atlas layout, renderer and batch membership.
2. Batch compatible scenery where practical. Compatibility includes material and sampling behavior, render state, sorting constraints and the ability to evaluate required instance inputs. Use independent rendering when required for sorting, a supported custom material or individual presentation control. Preserve effective appearance and phase when changing paths. Quality settings, culling and batch limits cannot alter owner state or silently lose an otherwise eligible placement.
3. Use the stock timing rates for the two verified scenery families. Other content uses its declared stock or mod timing profile. Derive independent decorative offsets from stable placement identity and animation profile. Resolve synchronized groups through a common origin and compatible group timeline. Avoid dependence on load order, iteration order or gameplay random draws.
4. Evaluate the visible frame from elapsed presentation time and the applicable phase. Offscreen frame updates may be skipped. Re-evaluate on visibility, residency or renderer changes using the current presentation time. Batch rebuilds and world-origin shifts preserve phase within the active session.
5. Support validated mod contributions for frames and replacements, timing, phase policy, synchronization groups, static substitution and rendering requirements. A deliberately static replacement declares its selected frame or asset and compatible placement. Missing required animation frames or an invalid selected replacement use DATA-022 recovery. Apply AV-001 optional decoration rules where the owner explicitly declares the placement optional.
6. Treat animation and representation as presentation outputs. Collision, harvesting, interaction and gameplay timing remain under their respective owners, including during a billboard-to-mesh swap. Preserve placement and gameplay state through either rendering path.

## Formulas, parameters and configuration

For a uniform loop of `N ≥ 1` frames at `f > 0` frames per second and elapsed presentation time `t ≥ 0` in seconds, use zero-based index `(floor(t × f) + offset) mod N`. A stable independent offset lies in `0..N−1`. A synchronized group uses its common time origin and phase under a validated profile. For declared unequal frame lengths, wrap elapsed phase by the total duration and select the frame whose half-open interval contains it. Positive finite durations and a valid frame sequence are required. An explicitly static profile selects its declared frame directly.

Configurable inputs include sequence, cadence, phase policy, synchronization, material and filtering compatibility, camera-facing rule, shadow and sorting policy, and declared rendering requirements. Stock Classic and Enhanced sampling follow AV-001. Batch limits and atlas budgets depend on representative profiling.

## Required data

Stable placement and profile IDs, world anchor and dimensions, frame references and rectangles, frame geometry or pivot metadata where needed, animation timeline, sync group identity if applicable, material and render settings, representation and requiredness from the placement owner, and resolved asset provenance.

## Inputs, outputs and events

- **Inputs** include resolved placements and assets, presentation time, active view and visibility, preset changes, and placement activation or retirement.
- **Outputs** include the active representation, geometry or instance bindings, effective frame and affected placement diagnostics.
- **Events** report presentation readiness, failure or retirement for the applicable activation. Decorative loops produce no gameplay events.

## Cross-system review

- **DATA-003 and DATA-022** govern validated contributions, replacement precedence, frame compatibility and explicit recovery.
- **AV-001** governs presentation presets, material isolation and optional omission. Atlas sampling must remain frame-safe under supported presets.
- **WORLD-004/006/011 and LOC** govern residency, placement and gameplay readiness. Preserve state and anchors across vegetation representation swaps. Only the responsible owner declares optionality and authoritative collision or interaction.

These rules are consistent with the reviewed approval boundaries. The renderer and batching strategy remain technical choices.

## Edge cases and failure conditions

- Validate frame order, frame rectangles and pivots, group compatibility, and finite timing. Missing frames require applicable recovery. An intentional static profile is a distinct valid input.
- Check neighboring atlas regions under mipmaps, translucent overlap, tall sprites near culling bounds and animated cutout silhouettes. Use compatible padding, representation or independent sorting where required.
- Ignore obsolete results after retirement, replacement or rebuild. A return from culling or a change in batch membership must evaluate the current phase without duplicating the placement or replaying frame increments.

## Save/load considerations

Reconstruct decorative frame and batch state from the restored placements, validated visual profiles and current presentation clock. Stable offsets remain reproducible. The decorative clock may start a new epoch on native load. Gameplay-bearing progress stays in its owner and follows that owner's restore contract.

## Technical constraints

Target Godot 4.7.2. Keep view and per-frame rendering work independent of gameplay timing. Resource ownership includes atlas, material and instance lifetime across loading and unloading. Measure batching and independent draw costs on representative areas before setting budgets.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Stock animal and light scenery animate at 5 and 12 fps respectively. Independent offsets are stable when insertion order changes. A valid mod group synchronizes as declared.
2. Culling and batch rebuilds preserve the frame expected from the active presentation clock when a sprite becomes visible again. They emit no gameplay events.
3. Compatible sprites can share a batch. Any sprite requiring a supported independent path retains its placement, effective frame and appearance when the rendering path changes.
4. Representative Classic and Enhanced fixtures retain ground alignment and clean sampled frames. A vegetation representation swap preserves owner state, identity and authoritative collision.
5. Validated custom timing and deliberate static substitution work. Invalid selected replacements and missing required frames use explicit recovery. Explicitly optional decoration follows the approved omission or fallback policy.

## Outstanding questions

- Verify stock pivots, facing, shadows, frame order and timing coverage with representative assets. Define atlas padding, sorting tolerances, profile schemas and performance budgets.
