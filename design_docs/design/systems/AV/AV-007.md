# AV-007 — First-person weapons and cast visuals

**Approval status — Approved, finalized v1.** The user approved Draft v1 on 2026-11-17 with “I approve”. See AV-007-A01 in the [gameplay decision log](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#av-007-interview-decisions).

## Gameplay purpose and intended player experience

Communicate the selected hand or weapon, readiness, strike, bow release and spell casting through first-person visuals. Stock sprites retain the classic look. Modders can supply sprite sequences or animated 3D models for the same action, with smoother motion and optional movement sway. The visible strike or release aligns with the gameplay phase.

## Confirmed requirements

[AV-007-D01 through D03](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#av-007-interview-decisions) confirm the classic default, compatible sprite and 3D mod replacements, configurable sway and optional-art recovery. AV-007-A01 approves the complete specification.

## Existing Daggerfall Unity behavior

In the local DFU 1.1.1 release snapshot, `Assets/Scripts/Game/FPSWeapon.cs#LoadWeaponAtlas` and `OnGUI` load and draw first-person weapon sprites. `GetHitFrame` returns frame 2 for non-bows and frame 5 for bows, and `WeaponManager.cs` checks equality with the current frame. `FPSSpellCasting.cs` aligns left and right hand sprites and signals release on frame 5. `Game/MagicAndEffects/EntityEffectManager.cs#PlayerSpellCasting_OnReleaseFrame` consumes that signal. These reference frame numbers describe the inspected visuals. Native action timing follows the approved owner profiles.

## Detailed rules and behaviors

1. Resolve stock visuals from the authoritative action and selected source. Use classic sprite profiles with applicable hand, weapon, material, direction and form mappings. Support validated mod sprite sequences, anchors and dedicated left- or right-hand assets. Mirror only where the profile declares that treatment suitable. Preserve the selected source and its owner-defined handedness.
2. Accept animated 3D models as first-person replacements for weapon and cast sprites. Profiles declare applicable actions and hands, view-relative transforms, material and animation resources and cue alignment. Allow authored meshes with transform or skeletal animation. Sample clips continuously where supported so visible motion can be smoother at higher render rates. Display-rate changes, extra visual frames and clip interpolation leave the captured gameplay durations intact.
3. Map the visible wind-up, strike or release, and recovery to the action's authoritative progression. COMBAT-004 delivers a crossed commitment marker once per eligible attack. MAGIC-004 owns checked spell release and recovery. A visual notification may identify an authored cue through the owner contract. Repeats, stale notifications, asset rebuilds and skipped renders produce no additional gameplay commitment. Cancel or transition the active visual when the owner cancels or settles its action.
4. Support optional view-relative movement sway through validated mod profiles. Use eligible movement signals to drive cosmetic motion and allow profiles to control amplitude, response and phase weighting, including disabling sway. Define the allowed sway range so the action remains readable. Sway leaves attack aim, reach, obstruction, projectile origin, interaction and character motion to their owners. Use an independent first-person presentation transform so visual movement does not redirect the gameplay camera or aiming inputs.
5. Compose first-person sprites and 3D visuals as part of the world-facing presentation, before menu and gameplay HUD composition. Support active AV-005 retro settings and AV-006 world effects through the applicable pass. Give the 3D path an explicit depth and clipping policy so nearby walls and unusual field-of-view settings cannot hide the whole weapon by accident or reveal gameplay-relevant objects through walls. Authoritative placement and obstruction checks remain with COMBAT, MAGIC and MOVE.
6. For unavailable art declared optional, use the profile's compatible fallback if available. Otherwise omit the affected visual, record an attributable diagnostic and let the action follow normal gameplay rules. A fallback visual does not select another weapon or cast source. Invalid selected mod replacements and missing required resources follow DATA recovery. Apply only a current activation's visual results.

## Formulas, parameters and configuration

Map eligible phase progress to the declared visual range. For a positive phase duration, normalized progress is `clamp(eligible phase elapsed / captured duration, 0, 1)`. Align the authored contact or release pose to the authoritative phase marker. Treat zero-duration phases through explicit visual mapping. Supported parameters include sprite timing and anchors, view model transform and clip bindings, alignment markers, sway amplitude and response, lighting and bounds, and fallback and requiredness policies. Validate effective ranges and applicable hand/action mappings.

## Required data

Action, source, hand and session identities, captured timings and owner phase state, visual profile and provenance, sprite or 3D resource references, authored clip and cue mappings, material and presentation settings, movement and view inputs, and declared fallback and requiredness.

## Inputs, outputs and events

- **Inputs** include selected source and form, action phase and progression, interruption and settlement, eligible movement signals, and presentation preferences.
- **Outputs** include the active sprite or model, sampled pose or frame, presentation cue and attributable readiness or failure.
- **Events** report applicable presentation cues and readiness. COMBAT and MAGIC own commitment, payload, costs and practice.

## Cross-system review

- **COMBAT-001/002/003/004/007/009 and RULE-015** govern interruption, hands, direction, committed attack timing, bow release and projectile geometry. **MAGIC-004** governs timing, release and recovery for casts.
- **DATA-003/021/022 and MOD** govern model, texture and frame replacement validation, provenance and recovery. **MOVE** supplies committed movement and authoritative aim inputs.
- **AV-005/006 and UI-004** govern world and UI composition. Post-processing and view-model presentation do not take input ownership.

A change of visual representation preserves the selected gameplay source and its action progression under the approved timing and hand ownership rules.

## Edge cases and failure conditions

- Test skipped frames, different display rates, interruption at release, duplicate callbacks, stale source changes and visual rebuilds. Commit a gameplay result only through the owning action.
- Verify left-hand alternatives, form overrides, mismatched anchors, missing clips and optional art failure. Validate assets and cue mappings before publication where detectable.
- Check close walls, extreme supported fields of view, camera motion and scaled world targets. Keep visual clipping and sway independent of gameplay aim and collision.

## Save/load considerations

Rebuild visuals from the owner-approved restored state and current resolved visual preferences. Do not persist raw animation resources, view sway phase or presentation callbacks. Respect owner rules for canceling incomplete actions and retaining committed state. Visual reconstruction does not replay strike or release cues as gameplay events.

## Technical constraints

Target Godot 4.7.2. Keep action progression separate from rendering and animation sampling. Scope temporal state and callbacks to their view and action lifetime. Validate supported model, material and animation capabilities, and verify the composition and clipping policy for both low-resolution and display-resolution world views.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Stock sprites reflect the selected hand, weapon, material, direction and form. Valid dedicated hand assets and 3D weapon or cast replacements bind under the same owner-selected action.
2. A 3D replacement can animate smoothly and show configured movement sway. Varying display rate, visual clip sampling and sway settings preserves authoritative phase timing, aim and action outcomes.
3. A skipped render, duplicate callback, cancellation or stale asset completion cannot cause an extra strike, spell, resource cost or practice increment.
4. Optional art uses its declared fallback or is omitted with a diagnostic. Invalid selected replacements and missing required resources use their approved recovery rules.
5. First-person visuals compose with retro and native world rendering and preserve HUD/menu behavior. Load reconstructs the visible owner state without replaying gameplay events.
