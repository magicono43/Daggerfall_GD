# AV-006 — Palette, depth and post-processing settings

**Approval status — Approved, finalized v1.** The user approved Draft v1 on 2026-11-17 with “I approve”. See AV-006-A01 in the [gameplay decision log](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#av-006-interview-decisions).

## Gameplay purpose and intended player experience

Preserve the original colors of classic artwork and let players choose how the finished world image looks. A player can keep full rendered color at 320×200, or use palette mapping at display resolution. The gameplay HUD and menus retain their approved composition and readability.

## Confirmed requirements

[AV-006-D01 and D02](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#av-006-interview-decisions) confirm the optional world color modes, sky control, five additional opt-in effects, validated mod extensions and optional effect recovery. AV-006-A01 approves the complete specification.

## Existing Daggerfall Unity behavior

In the local DFU 1.1.1 release snapshot, `Assets/Scripts/API/DFPalette.cs` supports classic 256-entry palettes and `API/TextureFile.cs#PaletteName` selects ART_PAL.COL for classic textures. Separately, `Utility/RetroRenderer.cs#InitLut` builds a lookup table by finding the nearest entry in its art palette for sampled rendered colors. `UpdateDepthProcessMaterial` selects the retro depth, posterization or palette material and the applicable sky exclusion. `OnPostRender` blits the retro target with or without that material. `Game/UserInterfaceWindows/GameEffectsConfigWindow.cs#AddCorePages` registers stock effect pages, and `AddConfigPage` supports extensions. These findings come from static inspection.

## Detailed rules and behaviors

1. Decode classic source imagery with its declared palette and transparency under DATA-022. Apply AV-001 material settings and AV-003/004 sky, weather and lighting during world rendering. Changing post-processing preserves source asset data and selection.
2. Offer mutually exclusive **Off**, **Posterization** and **Palette Mapping** at display and retro resolutions. Default to Off. Posterization reduces color levels. Palette Mapping maps rendered colors to entries in the selected palette, with a stock classic art palette and validated mod alternatives. An independent sky inclusion setting controls whether the selected color effect processes sky pixels. Include the sky by default when a color effect is active.
3. Offer independent opt-in **dithering, bloom, vignette, depth of field and motion blur**, each defaulting to Off. Declare per-effect parameters, layer scope, ordering and required render inputs. Effects requiring depth or motion data consume the applicable world view inputs. Sky exclusion from the selected color effect does not automatically change other effects' scope.
4. Run world image processing at the active world resolution before AV-005 HUD composition and final display scaling. Keep gameplay HUD pixelation under its independent option. Menus and their controls remain in the display-resolution UI pass. For the stock stack, apply depth of field, motion blur and bloom before final color reduction. Apply color dithering at the quantization stage when a color mode is active, and put stock vignette before that stage. Dithering is also selectable by itself through its declared standalone profile. Keep mapped pixels within the chosen palette at the output of the color stage.
5. Let mods add palettes, effect presets and compatible effects through validated registrations. Require stable IDs, declared settings, input buffers, layer scope, supported render modes and ordering constraints. Reject ambiguous ordering, dependency cycles and unsupported required inputs at validation where detectable. Apply DATA-003 precedence and the applicable MOD provider contracts.
6. Publish a coherent configuration for the active view when settings, capabilities or resolution change. If an optional effect or its resources fail at activation or runtime, bypass the affected effect and any effects requiring its output, report the effective state and keep the usable world and UI composition. A missing saved mod palette or preset follows the same preference recovery, with the affected color mode Off if its selected palette is unavailable. Required content uses its owner recovery.
7. Rendered color, depth blur and motion treatment have no authority over gameplay visibility, targeting, exposure, movement or camera input.

## Formulas, parameters and configuration

Palette mapping selects a nearest entry for the processed sample under the palette profile's validated color metric and deterministic tie rule. A lookup table may approximate that selection within verified tolerances and must still return a palette entry. Posterization uses a declared color space and positive level counts. Profiles declare finite strengths and supported ranges, with valid depth units and buffer conventions where applicable. Color space, metric, lookup precision, stock dithering behavior and exact default effect parameters require visual fixtures.

## Required data

Active view and resolution, world color buffer and applicable sky, depth and motion inputs, resolved effect and palette definitions with provenance, declared ordering and capability requirements, enabled preferences and effective effect state, and resource and activation identities.

## Inputs, outputs and events

- **Inputs** include the rendered world and applicable buffers, graphics preference changes, view and mode changes, resolved definitions and capability results.
- **Outputs** include the processed world image for AV-005, effective effect settings and attributable failure diagnostics.
- **Events** report applied or failed effect configuration for the applicable view. Post-processing produces no gameplay events.

## Cross-system review

- **DATA-003/022** govern resolved definitions, source palettes, validation and content recovery.
- **AV-001/003/004** supply material, sky and atmosphere inputs and establish gameplay separation. **AV-005 and UI-004** govern HUD composition, scaling, menu readability and input mapping.
- **MOD** governs provider registration and lifecycle. Graphics preferences and transient effect resources remain outside native gameplay save state.

The specification follows the reviewed artwork preservation, HUD and gameplay boundaries.

## Edge cases and failure conditions

- Verify sky exclusion at skyline edges and across views with different skies. Handle missing or incompatible sky, depth or motion inputs through the declared optional effect recovery.
- Reset obsolete temporal data after camera cuts, native restore and target changes. Reject stale work for a superseded configuration. Verify no residual motion blur from an old view.
- Validate empty palettes, invalid parameter ranges, unsupported passes and cyclic ordering. Bypass failures coherently and preserve unaffected effects and usable UI.

## Save/load considerations

Persist effect choices, palette and preset IDs and parameters as graphics preferences. Reconstruct view resources from the effective preferences and supported rendering inputs. Native saves contain no transient effect buffers or motion history.

## Technical constraints

Target Godot 4.7.2. Measure world buffer, lookup and effect costs at representative display and retro resolutions. Define resource lifetime and cross-view isolation. Preserve AV-005's final nearest-neighbor retro scaling and match UI layout and hit regions after configuration changes.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A fresh profile preserves decoded classic artwork colors and starts with all optional effects Off. Posterization and Palette Mapping each work at display resolution and both stock retro resolutions.
2. Palette mapping produces colors from the active palette at its stage output. Sky inclusion toggles the color effect on the sky while world geometry remains processed. UI and HUD follow AV-005 independently.
3. Each additional effect can be enabled independently. Valid mod palettes, presets and ordered effects work within their declared scope, and invalid contributions receive explicit recovery.
4. Failure of an optional effect or required render input leaves a usable world and UI and reports which effects were bypassed. Changing resolution or view retires stale buffers and history.
5. Repeated switching and native loading preserve preferences and leave authoritative gameplay results unchanged for the same gameplay inputs.
