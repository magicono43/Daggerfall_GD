# NPC-001 — Outdoor civilian pool

Approval status **Approved — finalized v1**

The user approved Draft v1 on 2026-11-10 with “I approve”. See NPC-001-A01 in the [gameplay decision log](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#npc-001-interview-decisions).

## Gameplay purpose and intended player experience

Populate settlement streets with approachable pedestrians during the day. Ordinary crowd changes should feel natural at street level. A civilian death leaves a vacancy until the player leaves and returns to the settlement's outdoor population area or returns to the street from a building.

## Confirmed requirements

The [gameplay decision log](../../GAMEPLAY_DECISIONS.md#npc-001-interview-decisions) records NPC-001-D01–D04. The default is daytime-only ambient crowds, view-aware arrivals and departures, vacancy refill on settlement re-entry or building exit, and DFU-style scaling by settlement size. The schedule, refill policy, cap and distance settings are configurable for mods.

## Existing Daggerfall Unity behavior

In the local 1.1.1 snapshot, `PopulationManager` samples city navigation near the player and schedules pooled civilians for activation. Activation depends on daytime, racial suppression and the distance or angle to the player. Beyond 120 Unity units the visibility gate permits changes. Within that range it checks whether the actor is outside the forward hemisphere. Navigation failures, distance beyond 150 units and night queue recycling. The billboard appears after the first completed tile move. The melee death path deactivates the object. Its slot can later be freed by ordinary recycling, so exact refill timing depends on runtime conditions. Sources are `Assets/Scripts/Game/Utility/PopulationManager.cs` (`Start`, `SpawnAvailableMobile`, `UpdateMobiles`, `AllowMobileActivationChange`) and `Assets/Scripts/Game/WeaponManager.cs` (civilian hit branch). See the [NPC catalog](../../../analysis/systems/NPC_FEATURE_CATALOG.md#npc-001--outdoor-civilian-pool).

## Detailed rules and behaviors

- Apply to ordinary ambient pedestrians generated for a settlement's outdoor population area. Placed actors and quest-required spawning retain their owner-defined lifecycles.
- During daytime, admit eligible new pedestrians up to the current population budget. Require a valid, ready placement. Block new ordinary admissions while beast-form suppression is active under MAGIC-029. Existing actors continue under ordinary lifecycle rules.
- At night, stop ordinary admissions and schedule routine retirement. A nearby civilian in clear view may linger until there is a permitted departure. An active conversation holds its civilian through routine retirement until the conversation ends. Re-evaluate eligibility before executing any deferred change.
- For routine arrivals and departures inside the configured visibility range, require the candidate to be behind the player or hidden by solid occlusion. Beyond that range, a routine change can occur in view. The conversation hold applies at any range. Authoritative death follows combat rules immediately.
- On a committed death of a civilian managed by this feature, reserve one vacancy until a qualifying return. Normal streaming, distance retirement, nightfall and daylight transitions within the same visit preserve the vacancy.
- A completed exit from the settlement's outdoor population area followed by re-entry, or a completed transition from a building onto its streets, releases its accumulated vacancies by default. Fresh pedestrians become eligible through ordinary admission rules. A transition aborted before arrival preserves the vacancies. A building exit at night or in beast form can release vacancies, with actual admissions waiting for eligibility.

## Formulas and parameters

`base_cap = 24 × clamp(floor(location_block_count / 16), 1, 4)`, where block count is settlement layout width times height in reference-equivalent blocks. This gives 24, 48, 72 and 96 at the corresponding thresholds, with 24 as the minimum. The cap is an upper bound. For locations with a valid profile, the effective admission limit is `max(0, configured_cap − reserved_death_vacancies)`. A refill release restores capacity, and placement availability determines how quickly that capacity is used.

## Required data, inputs, outputs and events

- **Data and inputs** include settlement identity, outdoor population boundary and block count, population profile, world-time eligibility, committed transition kind and endpoints, navigation readiness and candidates, player pose and occlusion, active conversation holds, form suppression, active actors and committed deaths.
- **Outputs** include initialized civilian availability, routine retirement, reserved vacancy and vacancy release. Consume committed transition and death notifications once per operation. Actor identity is valid before interaction or dependent systems can observe availability.

## Edge cases and failure conditions

- If no valid point is available, defer admission and retry as world conditions change. Recheck deferred placements when navigation, visibility, time or suppression changes.
- A stuck civilian can be queued for routine retirement and still wait for a safe opportunity. Leaving the loaded area ends scene participation under the world transition lifecycle.
- Repeated death or transition notifications preserve the original settled result. Guard conversion, routine retirement and repeated scene teardown create no civilian death vacancy.
- Moving across a streaming seam or cancelling a door transition preserves the current visit. A successful building exit can deliberately refill the streets, including when a player seeks new potential victims. Mods can choose a stricter refill policy.

## Configuration and balancing

Expose daytime/nighttime schedule, refill triggers, density scaling or overrides, visibility and placement distances, and supported retirement settings. Use validated configuration and population boundaries. Precise Godot distances, sampling cadence and navigation failure thresholds are tuning work. DFU unit values are reference measurements.

## Save/load considerations

Restore the settlement visit context and its outstanding vacancies before admitting the restored crowd. Reload alone preserves the vacancy and a save made inside a building still permits the subsequent qualifying exit. Restore durable death and incident facts through their owners. Crowd reconstruction must avoid resurrecting the killed actor, replaying credit or duplicating actors with durable references.

## Technical constraints

Target Godot 4.7.2. The draft recommends keeping temporary scene handles separate from actor identities, initializing actors before publication and clearing recycled state before reuse. Ordinary instancing and reusable objects are both viable, with the implementation choice depending on profiling. See [NPC-AR-01](../../../analysis/architecture/NPC_ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md).

## Acceptance criteria

1. An eligible small settlement has a cap of 24, with caps of 48, 72 and 96 at 32, 48 and 64 blocks. Mods can override the density.
2. Daylight allows ordinary admissions. Night stops new admissions and retires the crowd as opportunities arise. A configurable nighttime profile can allow admissions.
3. Nearby visible civilians stay through routine population changes. Candidates behind the player or behind solid occlusion can enter or leave. A distant civilian can change in view, and an active conversation defers routine retirement.
4. A committed civilian death reserves exactly one vacancy across waiting, dawn and same-visit save/load. A qualifying outdoor re-entry or building exit restores the capacity once, and a different civilian can subsequently be admitted. Mods can change which transitions release vacancies.
5. Beast form prevents ordinary admissions while existing actors and quest-required spawn policies retain their established lifecycle. Returning to ordinary form allows normal admission within the current budget.
6. An unsafe placement, cancelled transition or repeated event produces no duplicate actor, lost vacancy or duplicate death/incident record.
